= Inclined Plane Physics

== Introduction

This document explains the implementation of inclined plane physics in the `falling_ball_view.py` demo. This demonstration illustrates the fundamental principles of classical mechanics applied to an object moving on an inclined surface.

== Mathematical Concepts

=== Gravitational Force on Inclined Plane

When an object of mass `m` is on a plane inclined at an angle `θ` from the horizontal, the gravitational force decomposes into two components:

[source,python]
----
# Gravitational force on inclined plane (tangential component)
inclination_force = GRAVITY * self.ball.mass * math.sin(self.board.inclination * math.pi/2)
self.ball.add_force(Vector2(0, inclination_force))
----

=== Force Decomposition

The total gravitational force `F_g = m * g` decomposes as follows:

. **Tangential component** (parallel to the plane):
+
`F_t = m * g * sin(θ)`
+
This component is responsible for the object's acceleration along the plane.

. **Normal component** (perpendicular to the plane):
+
`F_n = m * g * cos(θ)`
+
This component is balanced by the support reaction and does not affect motion.

=== Resulting Acceleration

The acceleration of the object along the inclined plane is:
`a = g * sin(θ)`

NOTE: The acceleration does not depend on the object's mass, demonstrating that all objects fall at the same speed on an inclined plane (without friction).

== Code Implementation

=== Inclination Parameter

The `self.board.inclination` parameter is a normalized factor:
- `1.0`: Vertical plane 90° (free fall)
- `0.5`: 45° inclined plane
- `0.0`: Horizontal plane (no movement)

Since 90° = π/2, the angle conversion is: `θ = inclination * π/2`

=== Force Application

[source,python]
----
# Calculate tangential force
inclination_force = GRAVITY * self.ball.mass * math.sin(self.board.inclination * math.pi/2)

# Apply force to the ball
self.ball.add_force(Vector2(0, inclination_force))
----

=== Mass Cancellation in Calculation

One of the most interesting aspects of this implementation is how mass cancels out mathematically between force addition and acceleration calculation. Let's analyze this process in detail:

**Step 1: Force Addition (in falling_ball_view.py)**
[source,python]
----
# The added force depends on mass
inclination_force = GRAVITY * self.ball.mass * math.sin(self.board.inclination * math.pi/2)
self.ball.add_force(Vector2(0, inclination_force))
----

**Step 2: Force Application (in ball.py, apply_forces method)**
[source,python]
----
def apply_forces(self):
    # Sum of all forces (including our inclination force)
    total_force = Vector2(0, 0)
    for force in self.forces:
        total_force += force

    # Calculate acceleration: a = F/m
    acceleration = total_force / self.mass

    # Update velocity
    self.velocity += acceleration * DT * time_scale
----

**Mathematical Demonstration of Mass Cancellation:**

Substituting the values, we get:

`total_force = GRAVITY * mass * sin(θ)`

`acceleration = total_force / mass = (GRAVITY * mass * sin(θ)) / mass`

`acceleration = GRAVITY * sin(θ)`

**Final Result:** The acceleration no longer depends on mass!

This mathematical cancellation demonstrates a fundamental principle of physics: **all objects, regardless of their mass, experience the same acceleration on an inclined plane without friction**.

=== Educational Implications

This implementation perfectly illustrates why:

1. **A feather and a stone fall at the same speed** in a vacuum (or on a frictionless inclined plane)
2. **Mass does not appear in the final motion equation** on an inclined plane
3. **The code faithfully reflects physical reality** despite going through an intermediate calculation that includes mass

This is an excellent example of how programming can make abstract physics concepts concrete!

== Educational Value

=== Illustration of Physical Principles

1. **Mass independence**: Demonstrates that mass does not affect acceleration on a frictionless inclined plane
2. **Vector decomposition**: Shows how a force can be decomposed into perpendicular components
3. **Angle-acceleration relationship**: Illustrates how the inclination angle affects acceleration

=== Related Concepts

. **Potential energy**: `E_p = m * g * h` where `h` is the vertical height
. **Kinetic energy**: `E_c = 1/2 * m * v²`
. **Energy conservation**: Without friction, mechanical energy is conserved
. **Work and power**: The work of gravitational force converts potential energy to kinetic energy

== Possible Extensions

=== Adding Friction

To make the simulation more realistic, we could add a friction force:
`F_f = μ * F_n = μ * m * g * cos(θ)`

where `μ` is the friction coefficient.

=== Energy and Speed

We could calculate and display:
- Potential energy as a function of height
- Kinetic energy as a function of speed
- Experimental verification of energy conservation
